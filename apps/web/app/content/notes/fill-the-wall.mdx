Sam Cox (Mr Doodle) will cover an entire house: walls, floors, furniture, his own clothes. Not one blank patch, yet it never feels messy, because every stroke speaks the same language: black lines, round little monsters, one panel after another.

Apple does almost the opposite: lots of white space, one idea per screen.

The two look like opposites, but they share one underlying rule: **consistency**. Same line weight, same corners, same rhythm, so the full never feels cluttered and the empty never feels cold.

Here is how this site splits the work:

- **Structure gets the white space**: windows, lists and buttons stay restrained, with square corners, black borders and hard shadows, like the desktop of an old computer.
- **The background gets the doodles**: icons on the desktop drift slowly, get redrawn and light up when the mouse passes. The [Doodle Wall](/play/doodle) turns “no panel left empty” into a toy.

I picked gold and green. Gold is the sun at four in the afternoon, and Georgia Tech’s color; green is Atlanta’s trees, in a city that calls itself “the city in a forest”. Black and white keep them standing.
